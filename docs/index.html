What it does

flumi reads a coordinate-sorted, splice-aligned BAM and writes a coordinate-sorted BAM, so it drops into a pipeline between the aligner and the isoform quantifier.

UMIs the way dorado made them

dorado stores the UMI in RX with substitutions and indels anywhere in the tag. flumi re-aligns RX to the UMI pattern instead of cutting at fixed offsets, and keeps damaged UMIs, attaching them to their molecule rather than throwing them away.

Molecules without annotation

A molecule is its strand, 5′ end and UMI. UMIs are merged with UMI-tools' directional rule; merges beyond one edit are allowed only where the local molecule density makes a coincidence unlikely.

A representative that is right

Each molecule's read is chosen by majority-vote junction consensus among its PCR copies, then poly(A) completeness, then exonic footprint. PCR chimeras are dropped.

Fast and small

Two streaming passes; the first keeps ~56 bytes per read of the open loci only. 2.1 M reads in 19 s using 74 MB.

Install

Every release has prebuilt binaries with SHA-256 checksums: Linux x86-64 and ARM64 (glibc ≥ 2.17, RHEL/CentOS 7 and later) and macOS 11+ (Apple silicon and Intel).

tar xzf flumi-v1.0.1-x86_64-unknown-linux-gnu.tar.gz

Or build from source (Rust ≥ 1.88, a C compiler and CMake):

cargo install --git https://github.com/martinandclaude/flumi

From pod5 to flumi

Basecalling is the expensive step, so do it once. With --kit-name, dorado classifies the barcode and extracts the UMI (RX) and read orientation (TS) from the untrimmed read, then trims, and records the barcode in BC and the read group. dorado demux --no-classify splits by that record. Basecalling with --no-trim instead keeps the barcodes but loses RX and TS; dorado trim --sequencing-kit SQK-PCB114-24 adds them afterwards (that is how the public data below was processed).

# One basecalling pass: barcodes, UMI (RX), strand (TS) and poly(A) (pt)
dorado basecaller sup pod5/ --kit-name SQK-PCB114-24 --estimate-poly-a > calls.bam

# Split by the barcodes already recorded; no second classification
dorado demux --no-classify --emit-summary --output-dir demux/ calls.bam

# Per barcode: spliced alignment keeping the tags, sort, deduplicate
for bam in $(find demux -name '*barcode*.bam'); do
  s=$(basename "$bam" .bam)
  samtools fastq -T RX,TS,pt,qs "$bam" | minimap2 -ax splice -y ref.fa - \
    | samtools sort -o "$s.sorted.bam"
  flumi --bam "$s.sorted.bam" --out "$s.molecules.bam" \
        --gtf genes.gtf.gz --stats "$s.stats.tsv" --molecules "$s.molecules.tsv"
done
  • Input: dorado ≥ 0.9.5, trimming on, aligned spliced and coordinate-sorted, with RX/TS (and optionally pt) carried over. The BAM is read twice, so it must be a file, not a pipe.
  • A GTF is optional. Deduplication never uses it; it only labels genes and calls full-length transcripts.

Output

--mode filter (default) writes one record per molecule, the representative. --mode mark writes every record: molecule members carry MI, uN and uS, and all but the representative get the duplicate flag 0x400.

How well it works: simulated reads

Real data never says which reads came from the same molecule, so accuracy is measured on simulated reads whose names encode their true molecule. The simulator runs dorado's own UMI extraction on read fronts with nanopore errors, and adds PCR copy-number overdispersion, 5′ jitter, junction wobble, truncated reads, PCR chimeras and a gene with 20,000 molecules at one TSS, where genuine UMI collisions occur.

How well it works: real data

The public ONT dataset UHRR_HG002_2026.06 includes HG002 RNA sequenced with SQK-PCB114-24 on two PromethION flow cells, four barcodes each, basecalled with dorado 2.0.0 (HAC v6.0.0) and --no-trim. For each barcode we add RX/TS with dorado trim --sequencing-kit SQK-PCB114-24, align genome-wide to GRCh38 (no-alt analysis set) with minimap2 2.30 using the dataset's own wf-transcriptomes settings, and run flumi with GENCODE v49, the annotation wf-transcriptomes used. The barcodes are independent libraries: no molecule is shared between them (matching UMI and 5′ end occur at chance level), so they are replicates, not a truth.

False merges: the UMI-swap control

Every molecule is given the UMIs of an unrelated molecule on another chromosome, keeping its own copies' error pattern, and flumi is rerun on the same alignments. Molecules that share a locus now carry unrelated UMIs, so every merge of two of them is a false merge, measured at the real local densities.

Missed merges

In 5′ neighbourhoods of ≥ 100 molecules: singletons whose UMI is one or two substitutions from a molecule with ≥ 3 reads, against UMIs drawn from other loci (chance), 200,000 singletons per barcode.

How to read this

  • No molecule truth. HG002 cannot say how many molecules there were. These numbers show how flumi behaves on real dorado output at scale and bound its errors with controls; per-molecule accuracy rests on the simulation above and on the UHRR spike-ins below.
  • The checks are independent of flumi's code: every full-length call and gene label is re-derived from the representative's CIGAR and the GTF, and both error counts must be zero.
  • Dense loci are where the errors are. Over half of all molecules sit in 5′ neighbourhoods of more than 1,000 molecules; there, about 3 % of molecules are merged with an unrelated one, and a few true copies two substitutions away stay apart. A 16-base UMI cannot separate every molecule at that density, and flumi's density gate trades the two errors off.
  • Replicate agreement mixes biology, library preparation and counting. Molecules removing most of the excess variance shows that PCR copy number, not sampling, dominated the read counts; it does not show that every molecule is right.

How well it works: spike-in truth

The same dataset's UHRR libraries carry Lexogen SIRV-Set 4: 69 SIRV isoforms at equal molarity, 15 long SIRVs of 4–12 kb, and 92 ERCC transcripts spanning six orders of magnitude in concentration. That is the only truth in the data: known isoform structures and known input amounts. The published UHRR basecalls had been trimmed without keeping RX, so both flow cells (24 barcodes each) were re-basecalled the way this page recommends, in one pass with --kit-name SQK-PCB114-24 --estimate-poly-a and trimming on (dorado 2.1.2, HAC v6.0.0), aligned to GRCh38 plus the SIRV-Set 4 sequences, and run through flumi and the same controls as HG002. Only the transcripts actually in the mix count as truth; the decoys of Lexogen's over-annotated annotation do not.

How to read this

  • The SIRV comparison is the truth-based test of flumi's representatives, and it is clear: the junction consensus picks the right structure more often than either alternative, on every barcode.
  • These libraries are shallow (median 2.5 M reads per barcode, about 11 % PCR duplicates; ERCC molecules have about one read each), so deduplication changes ERCC and isoform counts little, and the spike-ins cannot show the PCR-noise reduction that the HG002 replicates do. What they do show is that grouping by 5′ end without UMIs saturates against real input.
  • Unequal counts of equimolar isoforms come from the library and sequencing (reverse transcription, truncation, isoform-specific capture), not from PCR copies, so no deduplication fixes them.

The evaluation scripts (downloads, SLURM jobs, controls and scoring) are not yet public.

Limitations

  • At high molecule density a 16-base UMI runs out of room. On HG002, 0.8–1.1 % of molecules are lost to false merges overall; in neighbourhoods of more than 1,000 molecules about 3 % end up merged with an unrelated one (mostly unrelated singletons one substitution from a larger molecule, which nothing distinguishes from a PCR copy with one error).
  • The density gate assumes uniformly drawn UMIs. Biased UMI synthesis makes collisions likelier than it predicts; lower the budgets if you suspect that.
  • Reads without RX cannot be assigned (5–6 % of reads on HG002): dropped in filter mode, passed through unmarked in mark mode.
  • flumi 1.0.0 labelled genes and called full length from the representative's genomic span, so genes inside its introns were counted; fixed in 1.0.1 (see the changelog). Deduplication was not affected.
